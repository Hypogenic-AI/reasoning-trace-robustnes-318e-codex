\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\para{Chain-of-thought reasoning and trace length.}
Chain-of-thought prompting~\citep{wei2022chainofthought} demonstrated that eliciting intermediate reasoning steps substantially improves LLM performance on multi-step tasks. Subsequent work explored how the \emph{length} of these traces affects accuracy. \citet{jin2024impact} showed that longer reasoning steps generally improve performance, even when the additional content is not strictly informative. \citet{wu2025whenmoreisless} formalized this relationship, proving an inverted U-shaped accuracy curve with respect to trace length and deriving a closed-form expression for the optimal length as a function of task difficulty and model capability. \citet{su2025underthinkingoverthinking} complemented this with an empirical characterization of ``overthinking'' and ``underthinking,'' finding a strong negative correlation ($r = -0.72$) between response length and accuracy on incorrect answers. Our work extends these findings by studying how the length--accuracy relationship changes under distribution shift---a dimension these prior studies did not examine.

\para{Overthinking and efficient reasoning.}
The observation that longer reasoning can hurt performance has motivated a growing literature on efficient inference. \citet{shojaee2025illusion} identified three performance regimes---low, medium, and high complexity---where reasoning overhead is counterproductive, beneficial, and ineffective, respectively. \citet{aggarwal2025l1} proposed L1/LCPO, an RL-based method for training models with controllable reasoning length, discovering that adaptive length allocation consistently outperforms fixed-length budgets. \citet{lee2025tokencomplexity} introduced ``token complexity'' as the minimal tokens required per problem, revealing sharp accuracy thresholds. Compression approaches such as C3oT~\citep{kang2024c3ot} and TokenSkip~\citep{xia2025tokenskip} achieve 40--50\% length reduction with minimal accuracy loss. Unlike these works, which focus on in-distribution efficiency, we study how length control affects robustness to distribution shift.

\para{Robustness of reasoning traces.}
Several studies have examined the fragility of \CoT reasoning under perturbation. \citet{wang2024nora} showed that noisy rationales---both irrelevant and inaccurate---degrade \CoT performance, with longer chains being more susceptible to error accumulation. \citet{vonrecum2026interventions} found that reasoning LLMs are generally robust to perturbations but that adversarial noise inflates \CoT length by over 200\%. \citet{li2025mirage} argued that \CoT effectiveness is governed by the distribution discrepancy between training and test data. \citet{liyubo2026consistency} demonstrated that extended reasoning traces induce overconfidence, causing confidence-based defenses to fail. Our work bridges the trace-length and robustness literatures by directly measuring how fixed and adaptive length policies modulate OOD accuracy and calibration.

\para{Adaptive inference-time compute.}
The idea of dynamically allocating reasoning budgets has gained traction. \citet{zhang2025reasoningbudget} surveyed adaptive approaches, distinguishing fixed-budget from dynamic-scaling strategies. \citet{chen2025demystifying} showed that long \CoT enables self-correction capabilities that transfer to OOD settings. \citet{ballon2026trajectories} developed a protocol for probing reasoning traces at fixed token percentiles, finding that accuracy monotonically increases with trace percentage. Our \padaptive policy differs from prior RL-based approaches~\citep{aggarwal2025l1} by using a simpler prompt-based mechanism with self-reported confidence, allowing us to study uncertainty-adaptive control without model retraining.
