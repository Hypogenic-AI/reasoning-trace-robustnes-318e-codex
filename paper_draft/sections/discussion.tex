\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\para{Interpretation of non-monotonicity.}
Our results demonstrate that the relationship between reasoning trace length and accuracy is fundamentally different in-distribution versus out-of-distribution. In-distribution, moderate reasoning (\pshort, \pmedium) provides the structure needed for multi-step problem solving. Out-of-distribution, additional reasoning steps appear to amplify domain-specific errors and hallucinations, causing the model to ``talk itself out'' of correct initial intuitions. This is most dramatic on \csqa, where \pnone (84\%) outperforms \plong (58\%) by 26 percentage points---suggesting that for pattern-matching tasks, reasoning introduces noise rather than signal.

\para{Why does verbose reasoning hurt OOD performance?}
We hypothesize three mechanisms. First, \emph{error accumulation}: each reasoning step introduces a probability of error, and longer chains compound these errors~\citep{wang2024nora}. Second, \emph{domain-inappropriate reasoning}: when the model reasons at length about an unfamiliar domain, it may apply in-distribution heuristics that do not transfer, generating confident but incorrect intermediate conclusions. Third, \emph{answer revision}: we observe that \plong frequently changes initially correct answers through excessive second-guessing, consistent with the ``illusion of thinking'' phenomenon~\citep{shojaee2025illusion}.

\para{The adaptive advantage and its limits.}
The \padaptive policy achieves the best OOD-near accuracy (56\%) and the smallest robustness gap (30 points) by dynamically allocating reasoning depth. However, its effectiveness is severely limited by poor confidence calibration ($\ece = 0.258$): the model reports high confidence even when incorrect, triggering the long-retry mechanism on only 4.4\% of items. With better uncertainty estimation---\eg probe-based methods using logit entropy~\citep{ballon2026trajectories} or learned verifiers---we expect adaptive policies to show larger gains.

\para{Confirmatory amplification.}
The confirmatory experiment on \mini reveals a striking amplification: OOD-near accuracy drops from 70\% to 15\% between \pshort and \plong. This suggests that stronger models, with greater capacity for generating plausible-sounding reasoning, may be \emph{more} susceptible to overthinking. The overthinking problem may worsen, not improve, as models scale---an important consideration for deployment of frontier reasoning systems.

\para{Practical implications.}
Our findings translate to direct deployment guidance:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt,topsep=0pt]
    \item Default to short or medium reasoning for most tasks. Verbose reasoning (8+ steps) should be reserved for known in-distribution, high-difficulty problems.
    \item For OOD-robust deployment, shorter traces are safer. The $70\times$ efficiency advantage of concise reasoning makes this the dominant strategy for cost-sensitive applications.
    \item Adaptive policies with confidence-triggered escalation offer a practical middle ground, but require investment in calibration to realize their potential.
    \item Monitor robustness and calibration jointly. High confidence and long traces do not guarantee safer behavior---they may indicate the opposite.
\end{itemize}

\para{Limitations.}
We acknowledge several limitations of this study. (1)~\emph{Sample size}: with $n=50$ per dataset, confidence intervals are wide and some comparisons (\eg \pmedium vs.\ \plong) do not reach significance. Larger samples would sharpen estimates. (2)~\emph{Prompt-based control}: max-token limits and natural language instructions are imperfect length controls; actual trace length varies within each policy. (3)~\emph{Single model family}: results from the GPT-4.1 family may not generalize to other architectures such as Claude or Gemini. (4)~\emph{Deterministic decoding}: temperature $= 0$ eliminates sampling diversity; majority-voting approaches may show different patterns. (5)~\emph{Self-reported confidence}: the \padaptive policy's confidence mechanism is a behavioral signal, not a principled uncertainty estimate. Probe-based methods would provide more reliable uncertainty quantification. (6)~\emph{No RL-based control}: prompt-based length control is coarser than training-based approaches like L1/LCPO~\citep{aggarwal2025l1}, which achieve finer-grained budget management.

\para{Broader implications.}
Our results suggest that the relationship between reasoning depth and generalization is fundamentally different from the relationship between reasoning depth and in-distribution accuracy. This has implications for how we evaluate reasoning models: benchmark scores achieved with extensive reasoning may not predict real-world reliability under distribution shift. More broadly, the ``more compute is better'' paradigm for inference-time scaling needs to be qualified: additional compute helps only when it produces \emph{relevant} reasoning, and domain shift can make extended reasoning counterproductive.
